Tony Effect Study

Does a Tony nomination or win boost ticket sales?

We followed 40 years of weekly Broadway grosses through three moments in a show's life: the weeks before Tony nominations, the weeks between nominations and Tony night, and the weeks after Tony night. The question: does recognition actually move the box office, or do the best-selling shows simply get recognized?

Summary

What a Tony is worth at the box office

Decision summary

Nomination and winning are followed by meaningfully higher gross growth, but the study cannot fully isolate the award itself. Winners were already pulling ahead before Tony night, so use these estimates as strong market associations—not as a guaranteed return from a trophy.

What a producer can do with this

Nomination day

Demand starts to lift. Raise prices on the best seats and hold back premium inventory.

Tony night: won

Lean into higher prices, longer booking windows and a possible extension.

Tony night: lost

Plan for a short tail. Make discounting and closing decisions within weeks, not months.

How we measured it

A before-and-after regression on 40 years of weekly grosses. For each show we took the change in its weekly gross (before vs. after nomination day, or before vs. after Tony night) and regressed it on whether the show was nominated or won, holding season, earlier sales, seats filled and show age equal. Comparing changes between recognized and unrecognized shows is called difference-in-differences. Results were checked with lasso and gradient-boosting (double machine learning) models, and run lengths with a survival analysis.

change in weekly gross (log) = a + b × Won + earlier sales + seats filled + show age + season

Step 1

Does getting nominated help?

We compare new shows of the same season: those nominated for Best Musical, Best Play or a Best Revival award, and those not nominated in any category. For each show, we measure how much its weekly gross changed from the 4 weeks before the nomination announcement up to Tony night. The window stops at Tony night so that winning isn't mixed in.

Does winning help, beyond being nominated?

How we checked our work

Who is included and what could go wrong

A "fake-date check" (statisticians call it a placebo test) asks: if we pick a date when nothing happened, does the method still find an effect? If it does, the method is picking up differences that existed anyway. If it finds nothing, that's reassuring.

Sample and checks

    Can we say the Tony caused it?

    Not with certainty. Nobody assigns nominations or wins at random: voters reward shows that are already strong and talked about. The checks rule out the most obvious problems, but some of the gap would probably have happened anyway. So we describe these results as a strong association, not a proven cause.

      Does the model choice matter?

      The same gap estimated four ways: linear regression, lasso, and two machine-learning models (gradient boosting and random forest). Small numbers give the 95% confidence interval.

      Technical method (for data readers)
      • Winning: difference-in-differences on log weekly gross, with production winners and same-category losing nominees, using 8 weeks before vs 8 weeks after the ceremony week. Controls: musical/play, revival, pre-period capacity and gross, run age, and year fixed effects. Standard errors are clustered by production.
      • Event study: unit and year×week fixed effects (which absorb seasonality and market shocks), with week 0 as the reference week, plus a joint Wald pre-trend test.
      • Nomination: difference-in-differences of new-this-season shows, nominated vs. not nominated in any category. Pre = 4 weeks before the announcement week; post = the announcement through the ceremony week. Controls: pre-gross, pre-capacity, run age and year fixed effects. Announcement dates were checked against Wikipedia for 14 seasons; other seasons use the median 5-week gap.
      • Run length: Kaplan–Meier curves of weeks open after Tony night (shows open within 13 weeks of the latest data are censored), log-rank tests, and linear probability models of "still open after 13/26/52 weeks" on Tony outcome, with pre-nomination log gross and capacity, log weeks already running on Tony night and season fixed effects (HC1 CIs). Shows whose outcome isn't known yet at a horizon are left out of that horizon.
      • Category groups: new-this-season shows running in nomination week get nominated/won flags for production (Best Musical/Play/Revival), acting (and separately Actor vs. Actress categories) and creative/design (direction, choreography, score, book, orchestrations, design). All flags enter one regression with pre-gross, capacity, run age and season. Each effect is estimated by OLS with year fixed effects and by cross-fitted double ML (5 folds, median of 3 splits) with gradient-boosting nuisance models; pairwise Wald tests compare groups. Honorary and special awards are excluded.
      • Fake-date (placebo) checks: the ceremony is shifted 8 weeks earlier for winning, and the announcement 5 weeks earlier for nomination.
